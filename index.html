How does the brain learn to control movements?

We are explorers, aiming to understand the function of the cerebellum. In primates, 80% of the neurons in the brain are located in the cerebellum, and diseases of the cerebellum affect every movement, and every thought. We study the neural computations that allow the cerebellum to control movements and contribute to the formation of thoughts. Our principal model is the marmoset, and our tools include neurophysiology and mathematics. As we better understand the computations of the cerebellum, we will build neural implants that can reproduce that computation and replace the diseased cerebellum.
