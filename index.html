How does the brain learn to control movements?

We are explorers, trying to chart a poorly understood part of the brain called the cerebellum. In primates, 80% of the neurons are located in the cerebellum, and diseases of the cerebellum affect every movement, and every thought. We study the neural computations that allow the cerebellum to learn from experience, predict the consequences of actions, and control movements. Our work combines behavior, neurophysiology, and computational modeling to understand the neural basis of how we move, and how we learn.

Research

Cerebellar computation

We investigate how neurons in the cerebellum represent movement, error, and learning.

Motor learning

We study how the nervous system learns from prediction errors and adapts movements through experience.

Neural control of movement

We examine how populations of neurons coordinate activity to generate precise and flexible movements.
