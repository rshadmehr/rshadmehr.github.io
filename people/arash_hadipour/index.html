Arash Hadipour-Niktarash

MD Postdoc (2005-07)

Google Scholar

Research

Arash joined the lab as a postdoc in 2005, after completing his MD and research training from the Institute of Physics and Mathematics, Tehran. He was the first student in the lab to use transcranial magnetic stimulation as a method to investigate the neural basis of motor learning. He subsequently completed residency training at Harvard University. He is now a neurologist in private practice.

Ideas developed by Arash

  • Distinguishing the neural mechanisms of acquisition and retention of motor memories. Arash helped show that the primary motor cortex is not required for acquiring a new visuomotor adaptation, but plays an important role in retaining the newly acquired motor memory. By disrupting motor cortex at different times after learning, this work provided evidence that acquisition and retention are distinct processes and that the contribution of motor cortex to memory is time-dependent.

    • Selected publications

    Hadipour-Niktarash A, Lee CK, Desmond JE, Shadmehr R. Impairment of retention but not acquisition of a visuomotor skill through time-dependent disruption of primary motor cortex. Journal of Neuroscience. 2007;27:13413–13419.
