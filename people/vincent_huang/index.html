Vincent Huang

PhD (2007)

Google Scholar

Research

Vincent graduated with a BS in Physics and Physiology from McGill University, and then joined the lab as a BME PhD student in 2001. He discovered that statistics of error experiences during motor adaptation can fundamentally alter how the brain learns motor control. He completed his PhD in 2007 and subsequently became a postdoctoral fellow at Columbia University in the laboratory of John Krakauer, where he discovered the role of reinforcement learning in motor adaptation. He then took up a position as Scientific Analyist at Thomson Reuters. He is currently a senior research scientist at Surgo Foundation.

Ideas developed by Vincent

  • Showing that the persistence of a motor memory depends on the statistics of the learning experience. Vincent showed that the nervous system estimates the stability of the environment during learning and uses this information to determine how strongly a newly acquired motor memory should be retained. When the environment appears stable, learning produces a more persistent memory; when it appears variable, the memory is more readily forgotten.
  • Revealing how motor memories evolve immediately after an error. Vincent studied what happens to a motor memory during the seconds following the experience of a movement error. His work showed that the effect of an error continues to evolve after the movement has ended, demonstrating that the passage of time between trials is an important component of the learning process.
  • Demonstrating the benefits of active exploration during motor learning. Vincent helped show that people can learn more effectively when they actively select their own training experiences rather than simply receiving a prescribed sequence of movements. This work introduced the idea of active learning to motor adaptation, showing that learners can choose actions that provide useful information and improve the acquisition of a motor skill.
