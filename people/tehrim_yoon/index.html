Tehrim Yoon

PhD (2010)

Google Scholar

Research

Tehrim joined the lab in 2015 as a PhD student in the Neuroscience program, after completing a double major in Physics and Chemistry at the Seoul National University, South Korea. Tehrim is the receipient of the Korea Presidential Science Scholarship, as well as a former member of the Korea Physics Team for the International Physics Olympiad. In 2010, he won the gold metal at the Physics Olympiad. In 2015, Tehrim was awarded the Samsung Fellowship to study abroad. His research focuses on using mathematics and psychophysics to describe neuroeconomics of human motor control. Tehrim's work showed that the theory of optimal foraging, which had focused on patterns of decision-making, could be applied to the question of how the brain controls vigor of movements. The experiments confirmed many (but not all) of the predictions of the new theory, suggesting that decision-making during foraging, and control of movement vigor, fundamental aspects of behavior during foraging, may be governed by a shared principle of control. He graduated in September of 2019.

Ideas developed by Tehrim

  • Establishing movement vigor as an expression of subjective economic value. Tehrim contributed to the development of the idea that the speed and intensity of movements reflect the subjective value of the outcomes they produce. Rather than being determined solely by biomechanical constraints, movement vigor is influenced by the expected benefits of acting quickly and the costs associated with movement.
  • Linking decisions about when to move with decisions about how vigorously to move. Tehrim investigated human behavior during foraging, examining how people decide when to leave a rewarding location and how quickly to move toward another. Her work helped establish a common framework in which reward, time, and effort jointly influence both decision-making and the vigor of the resulting movements.
  • Demonstrating that the subjective value of visual information influences saccade vigor. Tehrim investigated how the economic value assigned to visual stimuli influences eye movements. Her work showed that saccade vigor reflects the subjective value of acquiring visual information, extending the relationship between reward and movement speed to movements whose immediate goal is to gather sensory information.
  • Investigating how effort and the cost of time shape motor decisions. Through behavioral experiments and computational analyses, Tehrim examined how the effort required to move and the opportunity cost of time influence movement initiation, reaction time, and vigor. This work helped connect theories of economic decision-making with the neural control of movement, suggesting that action selection and movement execution are closely related components of a common optimization process.
