Michelle Harran

Research

Michelle joined the lab in 2009 as a research associate for Prof. John Krakauer, after completing her BS in BME from Johns Hopkins University. She graciously helped advance the research of many people in the lab, including Mollie Marko, and JJ Orban de Xivry. Her research interests include the role of the cerebellum in motor learning.

Ideas developed by Michelle

  • Characterizing how the motor system's sensitivity to prediction error depends on error size. Michele contributed to work showing that the amount learned from a movement error is not simply proportional to the size of that error. The nervous system changes its sensitivity to prediction errors as their magnitude changes, helping define the rules by which errors drive trial-to-trial motor adaptation.
  • Linking motor adaptation to changes in corticospinal excitability. Michele contributed to experiments examining how the corticospinal system changes as people learn to reach in a novel force field. This work helped characterize how adaptation of motor commands is accompanied by changes in the excitability of the corticospinal pathway.
