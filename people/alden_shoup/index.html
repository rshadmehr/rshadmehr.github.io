Research

Alden joined the lab in 2022 as a graduate student in the BME PhD Program. She received a BS in Bioengineering from the University of Illinois, followed by an MS in BME from the University of Miami. Her research focues on the neural control of movements in the cerebellar nucleus. She invented a new method to rejuvenate silicon probes, recoating them to reduce their impedance, thus providing a very cost-effective way to return silicon probes to a new condition and produce superb recordings from the brain. She was part of the team that discovered that populations of Purkinje cells in the cerebellar cortex organize via their potent vectors to compute a function that moves the eyes toward the target, then stops it as it nears the target. She was the first person in our lab to use optogenetics in the marmoset cerebellum.
