Jean Jacques Orban de Xivry

Postdoctoral Fellow (2007-2010)

Google Scholar

Research

Jean-Jacques joined the lab in 2007 as a postdoc, after having completed his PhD in the Laboratory of Neurophysiology at Université Catholique de Louvain, Belgium. Jean-Jacques mastered the use of transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) and transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) in our lab, and made important contributions to the theory of temporal discounting and motor control. His research provided new insights into the role of the motor cortex in motor adaptation and generalization. He subsequently was appointed Assistant Professor at Université Catholique de Louvain.

Ideas developed by Jean Jacques

  • Establishing the role of motor cortex in motor adaptation and generalization. Jean-Jacques helped show that the motor cortex contributes to the adaptive control of reaching, and that its contribution depends on how a perturbation is experienced during training. His work using noninvasive brain stimulation further demonstrated that altering motor cortical activity changes how newly learned movements generalize to other movements.
  • Showing that motor learning changes corticospinal representations. Jean-Jacques investigated how learning novel arm dynamics alters corticospinal excitability. This work helped characterize the changes in the motor system that accompany the formation of a new motor memory and provided physiological evidence linking adaptation to changes in motor cortical output.
  • Linking the value of reward and the cost of time to movement vigor. Jean-Jacques helped develop the idea that the speed with which we move reflects a decision about the value of time. Because the value of reward declines with delay, time itself imposes a cost on movement. This framework provided a link between principles of decision-making and the control of movement vigor.
