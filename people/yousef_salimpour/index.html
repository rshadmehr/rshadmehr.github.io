Yousef Salimpour

Postdoctoral Fellow (2010-2014)

Google Scholar

Research

Yousef joined the lab in 2010 as a postdoctoral fellow, after completing his PhD in Electrical Engineering with a thesis in cortical neurophysiology from the Institute of Physics and Mathematics, Tehran. During his postdoctoral period in our lab, he studied neural basis of effort evaluation in the brain. He made a number of discoveries regarding the neural basis of effort evaluation. In particularly, he discovered that in Parkinson’s disease, it was possible to use non-invasive cortical stimulation of the motor cortex to alter the perception of effort and improve the motor symptoms. He completed his postdoctoral fellowship in 2014. He subsequently became a Research Associate at the Functional Neurosurgery Unit of the Johns Hopkins School of Medicine. He was subsequently appointed Assistant Professor at the Johns Hopkins School of Medicine.

Ideas developed by Yousef

  • Showing that effort costs influence how the brain coordinates the two arms. Yousef helped demonstrate that the nervous system considers the energetic and motor costs of an action when deciding how work should be distributed between the two arms. His work linked bimanual coordination to principles of optimal control and action selection.
  • Revealing altered sensitivity to effort costs in Parkinson's disease. Yousef showed that people with Parkinson's disease assign an abnormally high cost to effort when selecting movements. His work provided a computational explanation for reduced movement vigor and helped connect dopamine-dependent motor deficits to the evaluation of effort.
  • Using non-invasive brain stimulation to alter the perceived cost of movement. Yousef demonstrated that stimulation of motor cortex could alter how people with Parkinson's disease evaluated effort and consequently change their movement choices. This work provided evidence that cortical circuits involved in motor control also contribute to computations concerning the cost of an action.
