Ehsan Sedaghat-Nejad

PhD (2021)

Google Scholar

Research

Ehsan joined the laboratory in 2015 as a graduate student in the BME PhD program after graduating with BS and MS degrees in Mechanical Engineering from Sharif University of Technology. Ehsan's research focuses on neural control of movements, with a particular focus on saccades and the role of the cerebellum. His research demonstrated that in humans, there was a link between reward prediction errors, which are thought to lead to production of dopamine, and modulation of movement vigor. He led our laboratory's efforts in behavioral training of marmosets and neurophysiological recording from the cerebellum. He discovered that during movements, P-cells synchronized their simple spikes to control the timing of deceleration. He also led the work to write an open-source, freely available spike sorter, P-sort, specifically designed for analysis of neurophysiological data from the cerebellum. His development of neurophysiological experimental methods and analysis software provided a foundation for much of the research in our laboratory. Upon graduation, he joined Neuralink.

Ideas developed by Ehsan

  • Demonstrating that reward prediction errors modulate movement vigor. Ehsan investigated how unexpected rewards and disappointments influence the speed of eye movements. His work showed that reward prediction errors affect saccade vigor, linking reinforcement-learning signals to the control of movement. He also contributed to research demonstrating that movement vigor is a relatively stable, trait-like characteristic of individuals.
  • Identifying the cost of correcting movement errors during sensorimotor adaptation. Ehsan investigated how the nervous system balances the benefits of reducing movement errors against the costs of making corrective movements. His work helped establish that adaptation is influenced not only by the size of an error but also by the effort required to correct it, connecting motor learning to principles of optimal control.
  • Revealing the role of synchronous Purkinje cell activity in the control of movement. Ehsan investigated how populations of cerebellar Purkinje cells coordinate their activity during movements. His work demonstrated that Purkinje cells exhibit precisely timed synchronous spiking during behavior, suggesting that cerebellar output depends not only on the firing rates of individual neurons but also on the temporal coordination of spikes across the population.
  • Establishing marmosets as an experimental model for studying cerebellar motor control. Ehsan helped develop behavioral training methods and electrophysiological recording techniques for studying the cerebellum of awake, behaving marmosets. This work established an experimental platform for investigating the activity of Purkinje cells during visually guided eye movements and sensorimotor learning.
  • Developing open-source tools for cerebellar neurophysiology. Ehsan led the development of P-sort, an open-source software package designed to analyze extracellular recordings from cerebellar Purkinje cells. By providing tools for identifying and separating simple spikes and complex spikes, this work made cerebellar electrophysiological data more accessible and facilitated reproducible analysis across laboratories.
